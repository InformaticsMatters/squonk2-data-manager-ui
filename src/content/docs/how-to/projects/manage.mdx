# Manage

A project's **Manage** section states the facts of that project and holds the changes you can make
to it. It is available to everyone who can see the project, so you can always read a project's
details even where you cannot change anything — if your access is read-only, the section says so,
and every unavailable change explains what it requires.

## The heading

The heading names the owning organisation and containing unit, then the project itself, and beside
it a row of chips: whether the project is **Private** or **Public**, and every role you hold in it —
**Creator**, **Administrator**, **Editor**, **Observer** — or **No project role** where you hold
none.

## Coin usage

**Coin usage** states what the project's subscription has spent and what it is allowed. Beside the
heading are the subscription's tier and type.

The headline figure is the coins used out of the coin limit, with a bar beneath it. The mark on the
bar is the **included allowance** — the point beyond which coins cost more than the tier's own rate.
Beneath the bar is the allowance in figures, the billing day, and the days remaining in the current
billing period.

Four tiles break that down:

- **Burn rate** - coins per day.
- **Predicted spend** - coins projected for this billing period.
- **Storage** - the storage size, captioned with the coins it has used.
- **Instance spend** - the coins execution has used, where that figure is available.

If the subscription has reached its coin limit, the card says so and the actions that would spend
more are disabled.

**View subscription** and **View charges** lead to that subscription in
**[Administration](/docs/how-to/administration)**, which holds the detailed ledgers.

## People

**People** holds the project's **administrators**, **editors** and **observers**, and is where you
add and remove them. What each role can do is described on the [Concepts](/docs/concepts) page.

Where you may not change a list, it is still shown — you can always see who can reach the project.

## About

**About** states when the project was created and by whom, its containing unit, its owning
organisation, and whether it is private. Beneath those facts is the control that changes the
project's privacy.

Evaluation tier projects cannot be made private.

## What you can do here

**What you can do here** answers the question directly, for the two things that matter most:

- **Change files** - whether you can add to and modify the project's files.
- **Run work** - whether you can launch workflows, applications and jobs in it.

Where either is unavailable, the reason is stated beside it — a role you do not hold, or a
subscription that has reached its limit.

## Identifiers

**Identifiers** lists the project ID, subscription ID, unit ID and organisation ID. Quote these when
you contact support.

## Danger zone

Deleting a project permanently removes its files and working directories. You must be a project
administrator to delete it.

The deletion runs across two services — the Data Manager project and the Account Server subscription
behind it — so it reports its progress, and if part of it fails it says what happened and what is
left to do rather than leaving you to guess.

## A view across many projects

**Manage** is the best starting point for one project. For a view across an organisation or a unit,
use **Usage & Inventory** under
**[Administration](/docs/how-to/administration)**, which is read-only.
