## Inspecting Usage and Quotas - a How To guide

### Project summary

The best starting point for one project is its **Manage** section: open the project and choose
**Manage**. For a view across an organisation or unit, use **Usage & inventory** under
**Administration**, which is read-only.

A project's **Manage** section states the following as plain facts:

- Whether the project is public or private, its creator, and its administrators, editors and observers
- The project tier (evaluation, bronze, silver or gold) and subscription type
- Coins used, the coin allowance and the coin limit
- Predicted spend, current burn rate, the billing day and the days remaining
- Storage size, storage coins used, and instance coins used
- The actions you can take on the project, with a reason given for any you cannot

Consumption is measured in Squonk coins (or coins for short). If the project's subscription has
reached its coin limit, **Manage** says so and the actions that would spend more are disabled.

### Project charges

**Charges** under **Administration** holds the detailed, read-only ledgers for an organisation, a
unit or a product. Charges accumulate over a period of a month (the billing period).

This is broken down into two sections, the first for processing charges (e.g. applications and jobs) the second for
storage (assessed on a daily basis). Information is presented monthly, allowing you to select the billing month you are
interested in.

<Image src={ledger} alt="Product ledger" />

Note in particular the _Charge_ column in the _Processing Charges_ table. This shows the cost of your jobs and
applications. You will see that not all have an associated cost.

**Subscriptions** under **Administration** lists the subscriptions behind your projects and datasets,
grouped by organisation and unit, and lets you drill down into any of them.
Note: subscriptions are Account Server _products_ - a Data Manager _Project_ is created as an
_Account Server_ _product_ - and the Product ID is still shown in the technical details, though the
distinction is not that relevant for normal users.
